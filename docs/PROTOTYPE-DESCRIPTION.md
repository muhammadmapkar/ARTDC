# Section 7 — Description of the prototype

RepairLens is an interactive mobile application prototype for car owners who feel uncertain when arranging automotive repairs in Dubai. Its classroom scenario follows a young professional whose 2022 Toyota Corolla stops blowing cold air. The prototype helps the customer describe the problem, understand a quotation, compare fictional garages, authorize costs and keep a repair record. The target segment is a preliminary assumption. **[Insert validated teammate research findings and citations when supplied.]**

The visual design uses deep navy, electric blue, readable type and large touch controls. A five-tab bar keeps Home, Analyze, Garages, My Garage and Profile available. The Home screen shows a selected vehicle and four direct actions. Users can choose among three sample vehicles or edit the manufacturer, model, year, mileage and fuel type. The selection is stored locally. Each step has a clear next action so a presenter can complete the journey without searching through the app.

The customer begins by reporting warm AC air. A guided form captures a description, when the issue occurs, warning signs and a follow-up answer. A photo or video picker is offered where the installed Expo client supports it; the sample issue works without camera access. Preliminary guidance gives possible causes in plain language and questions to ask a mechanic. Safety warnings appear for concerning symptoms or signs. The app states that a qualified mechanic must confirm any diagnosis. The guidance comes from a fixed mock service, not a live AI assessment or vehicle inspection.

The quote analyzer opens a fictional AED 1,100 example: AED 750 for an AC compressor, AED 250 for labour and AED 100 for inspection. It explains each line and separates parts, labour and fees. The Repair Protection Score is 65/100 because the example omits the part category and warranty. Its deterministic checklist measures quotation documentation only; it does not assess garage honesty or repair quality. Users can generate a message requesting missing details. Selecting a real file never triggers claimed analysis: the app explains that document extraction is unavailable.

Three fictional Dubai garage profiles can be sorted by price, sample rating, completion time or warranty. Their Trust Passports show repair specialization, quotation breakdown, part category, sample reviews and simulated verification indicators. This helps customers consider scope and value alongside cost. The profiles are not real businesses, and their prices are not verified Dubai market rates.

Quote Lock records customer approval of a confirmed AED 950 quotation and a separate decision on a simulated AED 300 additional repair request. Approval raises the authorized total to AED 1,250; declining leaves it at AED 950. A six-stage simulated timeline then leads to a completed invoice in My Garage, where approval history and warranty reminders remain visible. Quote Lock is a local record of authorization, without payment processing or legal enforcement.

The prototype uses React Native, Expo, TypeScript, React Navigation, StyleSheet and AsyncStorage. Screens, data models, mock services, state transitions and design tokens are separated so later integrations can replace demonstrations. Profile includes a visible Reset Demo action. A printable Repair Audit Card reinforces six checks before repair approval. Current limits include simulated AI and garage data, no real document extraction, no completed Arabic translation and native file selection that depends on the installed Expo client.
